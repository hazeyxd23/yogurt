DRAFT 2 - what computers used to be for
========================================

There was a time when a computer was where you lived your life, not where you watched everyone else live theirs.

You planned your week in iCal. Kept reminders on Stickies. Learned a song in GarageBand, sorted the holiday photos, wrote the essay, balanced the budget, finished the report. Then you closed the lid and got on with your day.

It was a tool for your life and a tool for your work, and it was quiet about both.

Somewhere along the way that changed. Most of what we open now is built to keep us scrolling.

yogurt is my small attempt to go back. It's a productivity web app that runs in your browser, styled like a Mac from 2007, and everything in it is about doing something with your own time.

For work: notes with links and backlinks, graph notes for planning a roadmap, a calendar with recurring tasks, and a LeetCode tracker for interview prep.

For life: a reading room built as a Minecraft enchanting library, guitar practice with a scale generator and metronome, and floating sticky notes for whatever is on your mind.

No feed. Nothing fighting for your attention. Just windows, a Dock, and your stuff.

Built with FastAPI, PostgreSQL, React, Vite and Tailwind. The demo video is fully scripted: Playwright drives the app, and a small renderer I wrote adds the camera work, captions and a soundtrack synthesised in JavaScript.

DRAFT 1 - feature tour (emojis removed)
=======================================

Remember when your computer felt like a desk?

Windows you could drag around and stack. A Dock along the bottom. Glossy blue buttons. Everything in one place, and none of it trying to sell you something.

I missed that, so I built yogurt: a productivity web app that runs in your browser but feels like a Mac from 2007.

Every section opens in its own window:

- Notes with [[links]], automatic backlinks and checkboxes that work mid-edit
- Graph notes for roadmaps and ideas: double-click to add, drag to connect, group and prioritise
- Floating notes that follow you between windows
- A calendar-style planner with recurring tasks
- A LeetCode tracker that lives inside a note
- A reading room built as a Minecraft enchanting library
- Guitar practice with a scale generator and metronome
- And dark mode, for when the lights go down

Under the hood: FastAPI and PostgreSQL on the back end; React, Vite and Tailwind on the front.

Fun part: nobody touched the mouse in this video. A Playwright script drives the app, and a small renderer I wrote adds the camera moves, captions and a soundtrack synthesised in JavaScript, all stitched together with ffmpeg.

And yes, the logo is an iMessage bubble that says "gurt:yo".
